# 03 — Codes, permits, and compliance

**Verified as of:** 2026-08-11

## Executive summary

EVSE installations must follow the locally adopted electrical code (often based on NEC) and local
permit/inspection rules. NEC treats EV charging as a continuous load. Article 625 is the primary
EV power-transfer article. Permits are local even when a state adopts a statewide code edition.

## Detailed findings

- AFDC: charging equipment installations must comply with local and state codes; permits may be
  required from building authorities.
- EV charging is treated as a continuous load under the NEC; contractors should apply the correct
  continuous-load rules for branch-circuit and feeder sizing.
- A site installation plan is required in many areas before work begins.
- NEC edition adoption and electrical licensing boards vary by state; always verify the adopted
  edition and who may pull the permit.

## Claims we can make

- "EV charging is treated as a continuous load in the NEC."
- "NEC Article 625 contains most requirements applicable to EV power transfer systems."
- "Permits and inspections are set by the local authority having jurisdiction (AHJ)."
- "Confirm the adopted NEC edition and licensing rules for the job address before work starts."

## Claims to avoid

- A single national permit fee or turnaround time.
- Claiming every jurisdiction requires the same EV-ready parking ordinance.
- Treating website "licensed and insured" copy as verified licensing.

## Known gaps

- Full 50-state NEC edition table needs board-by-board verification (scaffold in brief 10).
- Municipal EVSE checklists exist in some cities (e.g., documented San Jose / Seattle tip sheets on
  AFDC) but are not universal.

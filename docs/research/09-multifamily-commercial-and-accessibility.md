# 09 — Multifamily, commercial, and accessibility

**Verified as of:** 2026-08-11

## Executive summary

Multifamily common-area, workplace, fleet, hospitality, and public-site work belongs in the
commercial lane. Utility coordination, shared parking, billing, accessibility, networking, and load
management dominate. DC-fast is a separate capability lane. Do not rank residential electricians
against DC design-build contractors on one score.

## Detailed findings

- Common-area charging may require HOA/condo approval, allocation rules, and different metering.
- Workplace and fleet projects often need networked EVSE, access control, and utility service
  design.
- Accessibility and parking geometry matter for public-facing ports.
- This directory keeps `residential_l2`, `commercial_l2`, and `dc_fast` evidence separate.

## Claims we can make

- "Multifamily common-area charging is usually a commercial-scope project, not a simple garage
  install."
- "Ask who owns the parking, who pays for electricity, and how access is controlled."
- "DC-fast projects need different electrical design than home Level 2."

## Claims to avoid

- Using a home Wall Connector page as commercial evidence.
- Using a DC-fast project as home-charger evidence.
- Accessibility compliance claims without citing the applicable standard and date.

## Known gaps

- Local condo/HOA statutes vary; only cite when a jurisdiction is named in a primary source.

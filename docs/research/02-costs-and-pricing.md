# 02 — Costs and pricing

**Verified as of:** 2026-08-11

## Executive summary

Installed cost is driven by circuit length, panel/service capacity, indoor vs outdoor mounting,
hardwired vs receptacle scope, permit fees, and whether load management can defer a service upgrade.
National "average install price" headlines are usually marketing, not a primary dataset this site
can defend. Prefer cost-driver frameworks and link readers to utility/state program pages for
incentives.

## Detailed findings

- Equipment purchase price is only part of the project; labor, materials, and electrical upgrades
  often dominate variance between quotes.
- AFDC notes state and utility incentives may offset equipment cost; amounts and deadlines change.
- Time-of-use rates can change operating cost more than equipment brand choice for many households.

## Claims we can make

- "Quotes vary with circuit length, panel capacity, mounting location, and permit requirements."
- "Some utilities offer time-of-use rates that change the cost of home charging." Source: AFDC.
- "Incentive dollars and deadlines should be confirmed on the current utility, state energy office,
  AFDC, or DSIRE program page."

## Claims to avoid

- Publishing a single national average install price without a primary source and year.
- Publishing current rebate dollar amounts or tax-credit eligibility without capture date and
  refresh job.
- Claiming Section 30C eligibility after June 30, 2026 without a fresh IRS primary check.

## Known gaps

- No durable national installed-cost survey assigned to this build.
- Utility EV rates are local; do not invent city averages.

# 01 — Equipment and charging speeds

**Verified as of:** 2026-08-11

## Executive summary

Home charging is usually AC Level 1 (120 V) or AC Level 2 (208/240 V). DC fast charging is a
different class of equipment used mainly at public, fleet, and some commercial sites. Connector
standards in the U.S. include SAE J1772 for AC and SAE J3400 (standardized name for the connector
derived from Tesla's North American Charging Standard) for newer AC/DC combinations. Buyers should
match equipment to vehicle inlet, available electrical service, and where the vehicle parks.

## Detailed findings

- AFDC states most EV drivers charge overnight at home using Level 1 or Level 2 equipment, often in
  garages, with outdoor-rated gear required outdoors.
- Level 2 commonly uses a dedicated 208/240 V branch circuit. Exact amperage and breaker sizing
  depend on the EVSE listing and the load calculation.
- Multifamily charging often behaves more like shared/public infrastructure than a private garage
  install because of ownership, metering, and parking rules.
- SAE J3400 is the standardized designation for the NACS-derived connector; legacy marketing may
  still say "NACS." Use J3400 in current technical copy and explain the legacy wording when useful.

## Claims we can make

- "Most U.S. EV drivers charge at home overnight using Level 1 or Level 2 equipment." Source: AFDC
  home charging page, retrieved 2026-08-11.
- "Outdoor EVSE must be listed/rated for outdoor use." Source: AFDC, retrieved 2026-08-11.
- "NEC Article 625 covers electric vehicle power transfer systems." Source: NEC / AFDC compliance
  guidance, verify local adopted edition.
- "SAE J3400 is the standardized name for the connector derived from Tesla's North American Charging
  Standard." Source: SAE / manufacturer guidance; explain legacy NACS wording when useful.

## Claims to avoid

- Claiming a specific kW rate or miles-per-hour charge speed for all Level 2 installs.
- Treating public DC-fast stations as residential installer listings.
- Connector compatibility claims without current vehicle/OEM documentation.

## Known gaps

- Per-vehicle inlet support matrices change with model years; do not hardcode without OEM pages.
- Local AHJ amendments to NEC 625 are not nationally uniform.
